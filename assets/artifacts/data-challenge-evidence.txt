SCENARIO 1: ReviewPulse / data quality
A fictional sentiment-model team wants to train on customer reviews containing duplicates, suspected bots, inconsistent formats, and potentially misleading star-rating labels.

Palina's original response: "no, we have to label it as negative based on the wording"

My reasoning: I initially proposed removing duplicates and identifying bot reviews. When asked about a negative review with five stars, I chose to label it by its wording.

Feedback and follow-up: The coach explained how duplicated examples can inflate test results, prompted me about the order of cleaning and splitting, and suggested a before-and-after comparison. I agreed that comparing results could help.

Next steps: An area to develop is specifying the test set, metrics, and checks that would show whether cleaning helped. No model was trained or measured in this exercise.

SCENARIO 2: TalentBridge / bias & a new setting
A fictional hiring model trained on one company’s past decisions is being considered for a new country and a much larger application volume.

Palina's original response: "hiring decisions are country and company specific, so they might not work well in a new environment"

My reasoning: I identified the risk that a model might not transfer to a different country or company. After the coach raised gender bias, I suggested removing gender as a decision factor.

Feedback and follow-up: The coach explained proxy variables and introduced subgroup evaluation. After being shown unequal recommendation rates for comparably qualified applicants, I said the algorithm needed to change before launch.

Next steps: The next step is learning how to define and compare relevant errors across groups. Removing one input alone should not be treated as proof that a model is fair.

SCENARIO 3: Two hospitals / privacy & collaboration
Two fictional hospitals want to collaborate while keeping raw patient records within their own systems. Their records also use different coding practices.

Palina's original response: "yeah maybe if each trains their own and then combine results? nto sure"

My reasoning: I proposed removing or replacing identifying information. After a follow-up question about keeping records local, I tentatively suggested local training and combining results.

Feedback and follow-up: The coach connected that idea to federated learning, explained that updates can still leak information, and asked how to evaluate the combined model. I suggested testing results both hospitals could verify; the coach made site-specific evaluation more concrete.

Next steps: I need more practice turning that initial idea into a specific validation plan and considering compatibility, safeguards, and monitoring. This was a learning discussion using fictional settings, with no patient data or deployed system.
